Why Scales?

Scales are the gym for pianists. They build:

  • Finger independence — each finger gets strong
  • Evenness — all notes same volume and duration
  • Speed — muscle memory for common patterns
  • Key familiarity — know where sharps/flats are

C Major Scale (1 Octave)

Right Hand: Notes: C D E F G A B C Fingers: 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5 ↑ thumb crosses under Left Hand: Notes: C D E F G A B C Fingers: 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1 ↑ finger 3 crosses over

The thumb-under technique is crucial. Practice the crossover point slowly.

Scale Practice Method

Step 1: Hands separate, 4 notes at a time RH: C-D-E (stop) → practice thumb under → F-G-A-B-C Step 2: Full octave, one hand, slowly (60 BPM) Step 3: Up AND down RH up: 1-2-3-1-2-3-4-5 RH down: 5-4-3-2-1-3-2-1 Step 4: Both hands together (same direction) Step 5: 2 octaves

G Major Scale

One sharp: F#

RH: G A B C D E F# G 1 2 3 1 2 3 4 5 LH: G A B C D E F# G 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1

F Major Scale

One flat: Bb

RH: F G A Bb C D E F 1 2 3 4 1 2 3 4 LH: F G A Bb C D E F 5 4 3 2 1 3 2 1

Technique Exercises

Exercise 1: Finger Independence

Play on C-D-E-F-G, holding all fingers down except the one playing:

Hold C-E-F-G, play D repeatedly (finger 2 only) Hold C-D-F-G, play E repeatedly (finger 3 only) Hold C-D-E-G, play F repeatedly (finger 4 only)

Exercise 2: Staccato vs Legato

Legato (smooth, connected): C──D──E──F──G (each note connects to the next) Staccato (short, bouncy): C. D. E. F. G. (lift finger quickly after each note)

Exercise 3: Crescendo/Decrescendo

C D E F G F E D C pp p mp mf f mf mp p pp (soft → loud → soft)

Daily Scale Routine (10 minutes)

Minutes 1-2: C Major, hands separate, 2 octaves Minutes 3-4: C Major, hands together Minutes 5-6: G Major (or current new scale) Minutes 7-8: Contrary motion (hands go opposite directions) Minutes 9-10: Chromatic scale (all keys, one at a time)
