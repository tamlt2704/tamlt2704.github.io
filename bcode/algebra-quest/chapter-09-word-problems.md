## The Quest

In the Numeria Library, you find a vault of **Ancient Scrolls** — riddles written in plain language that hide algebraic equations. The librarian explains:

> "These scrolls were written before algebra notation existed. The puzzles are described in words. Your job: translate the words into equations, then solve them."

Professor Zap cracks his knuckles:

> "This is where algebra becomes a SUPERPOWER. Real life doesn't hand you equations — it hands you stories. You need to translate the story into math. Let me show you the secret decoder ring!"

## The Translation Guide

Here are the most common word-to-math translations:

| Words | Math |
|---|---|
| "a number" or "some number" | x |
| "more than" or "added to" | + |
| "less than" or "subtracted from" | - |
| "times" or "multiplied by" or "of" | × |
| "divided by" or "split among" | ÷ |
| "is" or "equals" or "gives" | = |
| "twice" or "double" | 2x |
| "triple" or "three times" | 3x |
| "half of" | x/2 |

## Examples: Words → Math

**"5 more than a number"** → x + 5

**"A number decreased by 3"** → x - 3

**"Twice a number"** → 2x

**"4 less than triple a number"** → 3x - 4

**"A number divided by 6"** → x/6

**"The sum of a number and 10"** → x + 10

> "Watch out for 'less than'!" Professor Zap warns. "It's sneaky. '4 less than x' means x - 4, NOT 4 - x. The 'less than' takes away FROM the other thing."

## Setting Up Equations

Now let's translate FULL sentences into equations:

**"5 more than a number is 12"**
→ x + 5 = 12
→ x = 7 ✓

**"Twice a number equals 18"**
→ 2x = 18
→ x = 9 ✓

**"3 less than a number is 10"**
→ x - 3 = 10
→ x = 13 ✓

**"A number divided by 4 gives 5"**
→ x/4 = 5
→ x = 20 ✓

**"Triple a number, plus 2, equals 17"**
→ 3x + 2 = 17
→ 3x = 15
→ x = 5 ✓

## The 4-Step Method

For word problems, follow these steps:

```
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
    │  1. READ: What are you looking for? │
    │  2. DEFINE: Let x = the unknown     │
    │  3. TRANSLATE: Write the equation   │
    │  4. SOLVE: Do the algebra           │
    │  (bonus) CHECK: Does it make sense? │
    └─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Worked Examples

**Problem 1:** "Maya has some stickers. She gives away 7 and has 15 left. How many did she start with?"

1. READ: How many stickers did Maya start with?
2. DEFINE: Let x = stickers she started with
3. TRANSLATE: x - 7 = 15 (started with some, gave away 7, has 15)
4. SOLVE: x = 15 + 7 = **22 stickers**
5. CHECK: 22 - 7 = 15 ✓

**Problem 2:** "A pizza is cut into equal slices. Each of the 4 friends gets 3 slices. How many slices total?"

1. READ: How many slices total?
2. DEFINE: Let x = total slices
3. TRANSLATE: x/4 = 3 (total split among 4 people = 3 each)
4. SOLVE: x = 3 × 4 = **12 slices**
5. CHECK: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 ✓

**Problem 3:** "Kai is 3 years older than twice his sister's age. Kai is 17. How old is his sister?"

1. READ: How old is the sister?
2. DEFINE: Let x = sister's age
3. TRANSLATE: 2x + 3 = 17 (twice sister's age, plus 3, equals Kai's age)
4. SOLVE: 2x = 14, x = **7 years old**
5. CHECK: 2(7) + 3 = 14 + 3 = 17 ✓

**Problem 4:** "A movie ticket costs $8. Popcorn costs $5. You spent $29 total. How many tickets did you buy?" (You bought one popcorn.)

1. READ: How many tickets?
2. DEFINE: Let x = number of tickets
3. TRANSLATE: 8x + 5 = 29 (ticket cost + popcorn = total)
4. SOLVE: 8x = 24, x = **3 tickets**
5. CHECK: 8(3) + 5 = 24 + 5 = 29 ✓

## Common Tricky Phrases

| Tricky phrase | What it REALLY means |
|---|---|
| "7 less than a number" | x - 7 (NOT 7 - x) |
| "a number less than 7" | 7 - x |
| "the difference between x and 5" | x - 5 |
| "the product of 3 and a number" | 3x |
| "the quotient of a number and 4" | x/4 |
| "consecutive numbers" | x, x+1, x+2... |

The librarian hands you three scrolls. Each contains a riddle:

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  SCROLL 1: "The Merchant's Riddle"           ║

║  How many gems are in my bag?"               ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SCROLL 2: "The Builder's Puzzle"            ║
║                                              ║
║  "A tower is 5 meters taller than twice      ║
║  the height of the wall. The tower is 31     ║
║  meters tall. How tall is the wall?"         ║
╠══════════════════════════════════════════════╣
║  SCROLL 3: "The Twins' Secret"               ║
║                                              ║
║  "Two twins have a combined age of 26.       ║
║  One twin is 4 years older than the other.   ║
║  How old is each twin?"                      ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════════════╝
```

<details>
<summary>Solutions</summary>

**Scroll 1:**
- Let x = gems in the bag
- 3x + 4 = 25
- 3x = 21
- x = **7 gems**

**Scroll 2:**
- Let x = height of the wall
- 2x + 5 = 31
- 2x = 26
- x = **13 meters**

**Scroll 3:**
- Let x = age of the younger twin
- Then the older twin is x + 4
- x + (x + 4) = 26
- 2x + 4 = 26
- 2x = 22
- x = 11
- Younger twin: **11 years old**
- Older twin: 11 + 4 = **15 years old**
- Check: 11 + 15 = 26 ✓

The librarian stamps your adventurer's passport: "Scroll Reader — Certified!" 📜

</details>

## Did You Know? 🌟

The word "problem" comes from the Greek word *problema*, meaning "something thrown forward" — like a challenge tossed at you. Ancient Greek mathematicians wrote ALL their problems in words (they didn't have our nice symbols). Imagine solving "the square of the unknown added to three of the unknown gives twenty-eight" instead of just writing x² + 3x = 28. We have it easy!
