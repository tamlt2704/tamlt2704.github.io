## The Discovery

You're hiking through the Whispering Woods of Numeria when something catches your eye — a rolled-up scroll wedged between two mossy rocks. It's old. Really old. The edges are singed, and the writing glows faintly purple.

You unroll it and see:

```
╔══════════════════════════════════╗
║                                  ║
║       x + 3 = 7                  ║
║                                  ║
║       Find x to open the gate.   ║
║                                  ║
╚══════════════════════════════════╝
```

"What's x?" you wonder. And then — POOF! 💨

A cloud of purple smoke. A tall figure in a star-covered robe appears, adjusting crooked spectacles.

## Meet Professor Zap

> "Well, well! Someone found my old practice scroll! I'm Professor Zap — wizard, mathematician, and terrible cook. That thing you're holding? It's written in **algebra**. The most powerful language in Numeria."

You stare at the scroll. "But what does the x mean?"

> "Excellent question! The **x** is a mystery number. Something is hiding in there, and your job is to figure out what it is. Think of it like a treasure chest — you know something's inside, you just need to figure out *what*."

## What Is Algebra?

Professor Zap waves his wand and letters appear in the air:

> "Algebra is just **math with mystery numbers**. You already know regular math:"
>
> `4 + 3 = 7` ← you know all the numbers
>
> "But what if one number is hidden?"
>
> `? + 3 = 7` ← something plus 3 equals 7... what is it?
>
> "In algebra, we use a letter instead of a question mark:"
>
> `x + 3 = 7` ← same puzzle, fancier writing

That's it. That's algebra. Finding the mystery number.

## Your First Puzzle

Let's solve that scroll together.

```
  x + 3 = 7

  What number plus 3 gives you 7?

  Think: ___ + 3 = 7

  You know that 4 + 3 = 7

  So x = 4! ✓
```

Professor Zap claps his hands:

> "You just did algebra! See? Not scary at all. You've been doing this since you learned to count — we're just giving it a fancy name now."

## Why Letters?

You might wonder: why use x instead of a blank or a question mark?

> "Three reasons," says Professor Zap, holding up three sparkly fingers:
>
> 1. **Letters are easier to write** in longer problems
> 2. **You can have more than one mystery** — use x, y, z, or any letter
> 3. **It looks cool.** (This is the most important reason.)

## A Quick Taste

Here are a few more puzzles. Don't worry about "rules" yet — just think about what number makes each one true:

**Puzzle 1:** `x + 2 = 5`
Think: what plus 2 equals 5? → x = 3 ✓

**Puzzle 2:** `x + 10 = 15`
Think: what plus 10 equals 15? → x = 5 ✓

**Puzzle 3:** `x - 1 = 6`
Think: what minus 1 equals 6? → x = 7 ✓

**Puzzle 4:** `2 × x = 8`
Think: 2 times what equals 8? → x = 4 ✓

## Try It! 🎯

See if you can figure out the mystery number in each one:

1. `x + 4 = 9`
2. `x - 2 = 5`
3. `x + 7 = 7`
4. `3 × x = 12`
5. `x + 1 = 100`

<details>
<summary>Check Your Answers</summary>

1. x = 5 (because 5 + 4 = 9)
2. x = 7 (because 7 - 2 = 5)
3. x = 0 (because 0 + 7 = 7 — zero is a valid answer!)
4. x = 4 (because 3 × 4 = 12)
5. x = 99 (because 99 + 1 = 100)

</details>

## The Quest Begins

Professor Zap rolls up the scroll and tucks it into your backpack.

> "That scroll is just the beginning. Numeria is full of locked doors, sleeping dragons, and hidden treasures — all protected by algebra puzzles. The good news? I'll teach you everything you need. The bad news? My teaching style involves a lot of bad puns. You've been warned."

He points down a winding path through the forest.

> "Chapter 1 awaits. We're going to learn about **mystery boxes** — what mathematicians call *variables*. Once you understand those, you'll be able to read any equation in Numeria."

## Level Up! 📈

What you learned:
- Algebra is math with mystery numbers (variables)
- The letter **x** (or any letter) represents an unknown number
- You solve algebra by figuring out what number the letter stands for
- You've actually been doing algebra-style thinking your whole life

## Did You Know? 🌟

The word "algebra" comes from an Arabic book written around 820 AD by a mathematician named **al-Khwarizmi**. The Arabic title included the word *al-jabr*, which means "restoring" or "completing." So when you solve for x, you're literally *completing* the puzzle — just like adventurers have been doing for over 1,200 years!
