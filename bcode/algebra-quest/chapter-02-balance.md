## The Quest

You reach the **Dragon's Gate** — a massive stone door with a golden scale mounted on it. The scale has two sides, and they must be perfectly balanced to open the gate. Professor Zap examines it closely.

> "Ah, a balance puzzle! The rule is simple: whatever you do to one side, you MUST do to the other. Keep it balanced, and the gate opens. Tip it, and... well, let's not find out what happens."

## Equations Are Balance Scales

An **equation** is like a balance scale. The left side equals the right side — they're perfectly balanced.

```
    The Balance Scale:

        ┌───────┐         ┌───────┐
        │ x + 5 │    =    │  12   │
        └───┬───┘         └───┬───┘
            │                 │
       ─────┴─────────────────┴─────
                    ⚖️
```

The `=` sign means "these two sides weigh the same." Your job: figure out what x is without tipping the scale.

## The Golden Rule

> "Here's the most important rule in all of algebra," Professor Zap says, his eyes twinkling:
>
> **Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other side.**
>
> Add 3 to the left? Add 3 to the right.
> Subtract 7 from the right? Subtract 7 from the left.
> The scale must ALWAYS stay balanced."

This is how we "unwrap" the mystery number — by carefully removing things from both sides until x is alone.

## Solving with Subtraction

**Problem:** x + 5 = 12

Goal: get x alone on one side.

The +5 is stuck to x. To remove it, subtract 5. But remember the golden rule — subtract 5 from BOTH sides:

```
        ┌───────┐         ┌───────┐
        │ x + 5 │    =    │  12   │
        └───┬───┘         └───┬───┘
            │                 │
       ─────┴─────────────────┴─────
                    ⚖️

    Subtract 5 from both sides:

        ┌───────┐         ┌───────┐
        │   x   │    =    │   7   │
        └───┬───┘         └───┬───┘
            │                 │
       ─────┴─────────────────┴─────
                    ⚖️
```

**x = 7** ✓

Check: does 7 + 5 = 12? Yes! The scale balances.

## Solving with Addition

**Problem:** x - 3 = 10

The -3 is stuck to x. To remove it, add 3 to both sides:

```
    x - 3 = 10
    x - 3 + 3 = 10 + 3    ← add 3 to both sides
    x = 13                  ← the -3 and +3 cancel out!
```

Check: does 13 - 3 = 10? Yes! ✓

## More Examples

**Example 1:** x + 9 = 15
```
    x + 9 = 15
    x + 9 - 9 = 15 - 9    ← subtract 9 from both sides
    x = 6 ✓
```

**Example 2:** x - 7 = 2
```
    x - 7 = 2
    x - 7 + 7 = 2 + 7     ← add 7 to both sides
    x = 9 ✓
```

**Example 3:** x + 20 = 20
```
    x + 20 = 20
    x + 20 - 20 = 20 - 20  ← subtract 20 from both sides
    x = 0 ✓
```

Zero is a perfectly valid answer! Don't be afraid of it.

## The Pattern

Notice the pattern?
- If the equation has **+ something**, you **subtract** it from both sides
- If the equation has **- something**, you **add** it to both sides

You're doing the **opposite operation** to undo what's been done to x. Mathematicians call this the **inverse operation**.

| To undo... | Do the opposite... |
|---|---|
| + 5 | - 5 |
| - 3 | + 3 |
| + 100 | - 100 |
| - 42 | + 42 |

## The Dragon's Gate Quest 🐉

The gate has three scales. You must balance all three to open it:

```
╔══════════════════════════════════════╗
║  Scale 1:  x + 8 = 20               ║
║  Scale 2:  y - 4 = 11               ║
║  Scale 3:  z + x = 30               ║
║                                      ║
║  Enter the code: x, y, z            ║
╚══════════════════════════════════════╝
```

Hint: solve them in order! Scale 3 uses your answer from Scale 1.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

- Scale 1: x + 8 = 20 → x = 12
- Scale 2: y - 4 = 11 → y = 15
- Scale 3: z + 12 = 30 → z = 18
- **The code is: 12, 15, 18**

The gate rumbles open! Beyond it, you see a winding staircase leading up to the Multiplication Tower...

</details>

## Level Up! 📈

What you learned:
- An **equation** is like a balance scale — both sides are equal
- The **golden rule**: do the same thing to both sides
- To undo addition, subtract from both sides
- To undo subtraction, add to both sides
- Always **check** your answer by plugging it back in

## Did You Know? 🌟

The equals sign (=) was invented in 1557 by a Welsh mathematician named Robert Recorde. He chose two parallel lines because "no two things can be more equal." Before that, people just wrote "is equal to" in words every single time. Imagine how long your homework would be!
