## The Strategy

One product is a single point of failure. If it dies — competitor launches, market shifts, platform changes — your side income goes to zero overnight. The portfolio approach means running 3-5 small products simultaneously, killing losers fast, and doubling down on winners.

This isn't about spreading yourself thin. It's about diversification — the same principle that makes index funds smarter than individual stocks.

**The portfolio mindset:**
- No single product needs to earn $5K/mo
- Five products at $1K/mo each = same income, way less risk
- Kill anything that isn't growing after 3-6 months
- Double down on what's working (more content, better pricing, new features)
- Each product teaches you something that makes the next one easier

**Why one product is risky:**
- Google algorithm change kills your traffic
- A funded competitor launches a free version
- Your platform (Heroku, Twitter API) changes pricing
- You burn out on the same problem for 3 years
- Market shrinks or shifts

## The Math

**Portfolio allocation (10 hours/week):**

| Product | Stage | Hours/Week | Revenue |
|---------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Micro-SaaS (mature) | Maintenance | 1-2 | $1,500/mo |
| Template pack | Passive | 0.5 | $800/mo |
| API product | Growing | 3-4 | $600/mo |
| Newsletter | Building | 2-3 | $200/mo |
| New experiment | Validating | 2-3 | $0 |
| **Total** | | **9-12** | **$3,100/mo** |

**The lifecycle of a product in your portfolio:**
1. **Experiment (weeks 1-4):** Validate with minimal effort. Kill if no signal.
2. **Build (months 1-3):** MVP, first customers, product-market fit.
3. **Grow (months 3-12):** Marketing, pricing optimization, feature requests.
4. **Maintain (year 1+):** Minimal time, steady revenue. Cash cow.
5. **Sunset (when needed):** Revenue declining, no longer worth the time. Sell or shut down.

**When to kill a product:**
- 3 months in, zero paying customers despite marketing effort
- Revenue declining for 3+ consecutive months with no clear fix
- You dread working on it (life's too short)
- The market has fundamentally changed

**When to double down:**
- Organic growth without marketing effort
- Customers asking for more features (and willing to pay)
- Low churn, high satisfaction
- Clear path to 2-3x current revenue

## The Strategy: Compound Growth Over 2-3 Years

**Year 1: Foundation**
- Launch 2-3 experiments
- Kill 1-2, keep 1-2 that show promise
- Target: $500-1,500/mo total

**Year 2: Growth**
- Mature products move to maintenance mode
- Launch 1-2 new experiments with lessons learned
- Target: $2,000-4,000/mo total

**Year 3: Compounding**
- Portfolio of 3-5 products, most in maintenance
- New experiments are faster (you know what works)
- Target: $4,000-8,000/mo total

**The long game math:**
- Year 1: $1,000/mo × 12 = $12,000
- Year 2: $3,000/mo × 12 = $36,000
- Year 3: $5,000/mo × 12 = $60,000
- **3-year total: $108,000** in side income

**Selling products you've outgrown:**
- Marketplaces: MicroAcquire (now Acquire.com), Flippa
- Typical multiples: 2-4x annual revenue for SaaS
- A $1,000/mo product sells for $24,000-48,000
- Use the cash to fund your next experiment or invest

- [ ] List all your current income experiments (even dormant ones)

- [ ] Kill one thing that's not working (free up time for what is)
- [ ] Identify your highest-ROI product and allocate more time to it

## Time Required

**Portfolio review:** 1 hour/month (assess what's working)
**Killing a product:** 1-2 hours (sunset gracefully, notify customers)
**Launching a new experiment:** 5-10 hours (you're faster now)
**Ongoing management:** Same 5-10 hours/week, just distributed differently

## Real Examples

- **Pieter Levels:** Runs 10+ products simultaneously (Nomad List, Remote OK, Photo AI, etc.). Most are in maintenance mode. Combined revenue: $200K+/month. Started with small bets.
- **Tony Dinh:** Runs multiple small products (TypingMind, BlackMagic, DevUtils). Each earns $1-10K/mo. Portfolio approach means no single failure kills his income.

- **Many anonymous indie hackers:** Run 2-4 small SaaS tools earning $500-2000/mo each. Total portfolio: $3-8K/mo. Nobody knows their names, and that's fine.

## Pitfalls

- **Pitfall #1: Starting 5 things at once.** Sequential, not parallel. Get one to maintenance mode before starting the next.
- **Pitfall #2: Emotional attachment to failing products.** If it's not working after 3-6 months of real effort, kill it. Your time is worth more.
- **Pitfall #3: Neglecting winners to chase new ideas.** "Shiny object syndrome" is real. Your boring $1K/mo product deserves attention before your exciting $0/mo idea.
- **Pitfall #4: Not tracking time per product.** You can't optimize what you don't measure. Know where your hours go.
- **Pitfall #5: Forgetting the goal.** The goal is financial freedom and family time — not running a mini-empire. If 2 products at $2,500/mo gives you what you need, stop there.
