## The Strategy

You've built something that earns money. Now the question is: how do you grow revenue without growing your time commitment? The answer isn't "work harder" — it's pricing strategy, reducing churn, and letting your existing customers pay you more.

**The three levers of growth (without more hours):**
1. **Charge more** (raise prices, add tiers)
2. **Keep customers longer** (reduce churn with annual plans)
3. **Get more from existing customers** (upsells, usage growth)

None of these require building new features or spending more time. They require making better decisions about the business you already have.

## The Math

**The power of annual pricing:**
- Monthly: $20/mo × 100 customers = $2,000/mo, but 8% monthly churn means you lose 8 customers/month
- Annual: $200/year (2 months free) × 100 customers = $20,000 upfront, churn drops to ~30%/year

**Annual pricing benefits:**
- Cash upfront (fund your next product)
- Lower churn (people forget about annual subscriptions)
- Less payment failure (one charge vs. twelve)
- Higher lifetime value

**Pricing tier math:**

| Tier | Price | Customers | Revenue |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Starter | $19/mo | 50 | $950 |
| Pro | $49/mo | 30 | $1,470 |
| Business | $149/mo | 10 | $1,490 |
| **Total** | | **90** | **$3,910/mo** |

vs. single tier at $29/mo × 90 = $2,610/mo. Same customers, $1,300 more revenue.

**When to raise prices:**
- Always sooner than you think
- If no one complains about your price, it's too low
- If your close rate is above 80%, you're too cheap
- Grandfather existing customers, raise for new ones

## The Strategy: The "1000 True Fans" Math

You don't need millions of users. You need a small number of people who value what you build.

**$5,000/month scenarios:**
- 100 customers × $50/mo = $5,000
- 50 customers × $100/mo = $5,000
- 250 customers × $20/mo = $5,000
- 33 customers × $150/mo = $5,000

**Reducing support load (so growth doesn't eat your time):**
- Self-serve everything (billing, plan changes, cancellation)
- Documentation answers 80% of questions
- Canned responses for the remaining 20%
- Community forum (customers help each other)
- Office hours instead of 1:1 support (batch your time)

**Pricing tiers that self-select:**
- **Starter:** Limited features, perfect for individuals
- **Pro:** Full features, for serious users (this is where most revenue comes from)
- **Business:** Priority support + team features, for companies with budgets
- The gap between tiers should be 2-3x (not 1.5x)

## Action Items

- [ ] Add annual pricing with a 2-month discount (if you haven't already)
- [ ] Review your pricing — when did you last raise it? Raise it 20-30% for new customers.
- [ ] Add at least one higher tier (even if it's just "priority support + logo on site")
- [ ] Email existing monthly customers offering annual at a discount
- [ ] Identify your top 3 support questions and add them to docs/FAQ
- [ ] Set up a "cancellation survey" to understand why people leave

## Time Required

**Pricing changes:** 2-3 hours (one session)
**Adding annual billing:** 1-2 hours (Stripe makes this easy)
**Documentation improvements:** 2-3 hours
**Ongoing:** Same time as before — the point is growth without more hours

## Real Examples

- **Basecamp:** Raised prices multiple times over 20 years. Grandfathered existing customers. Revenue grew without adding features.
- **ConvertKit:** Went from $29/mo single tier to $29/$59/$119 tiers. Revenue doubled without doubling customers.
- **Solo SaaS devs on Twitter:** Multiple report that switching from monthly-only to annual+monthly increased revenue 30-40% with zero additional work.
- **Indie hacker pricing experiments:** One dev raised prices from $9 to $29/mo. Lost 10% of customers but tripled revenue. Net positive in every way (less support too).

## Pitfalls

- **Pitfall #1: Fear of raising prices.** Your current customers are grandfathered. New customers don't know the old price. Just do it.
- **Pitfall #2: Too many tiers.** Three is ideal. Five is confusing. Two leaves money on the table.
- **Pitfall #3: No annual option.** You're leaving 30-40% revenue on the table and dealing with unnecessary churn.
- **Pitfall #4: Scaling support linearly.** If every new customer means more support time, you'll hit a wall. Invest in docs and self-serve.
- **Pitfall #5: Adding features to justify higher prices.** You don't need more features. You need better positioning and confidence in your value.
