## The Strategy

You have a job you don't hate, a family you love, and maybe 5-10 hours per week of discretionary time. This course is about turning those stolen hours into $500-5000/month without burning out, neglecting your kids, or quitting your day job.

This isn't a "hustle culture" playbook. It's a system for developers who want financial breathing room — pay off debt faster, save for a house, or just stop worrying about one unexpected expense.

**Why developers have an unfair advantage:**
- You can build things that scale without hiring anyone
- Your "boring" work problems are someone else's expensive pain point
- You understand automation (your product can sell while you sleep)
- Technical moats are real — non-devs can't easily copy your tools

**The "Small Bets" Philosophy:**
- Never go all-in on one idea
- Launch fast, validate faster, kill losers quickly
- Multiple $500/mo products beat one fragile $5000/mo product
- Compound small wins over 2-3 years

## The Math

**Where do your 5-10 hours come from?**

| Time Slot | Hours/Week | Notes |
|-----------|-----------|-------|
| Early mornings (5:30-6:30am) | 3-5 | Before kids wake up |
| Naptime (weekends) | 2-3 | Toddler naps = gold |
| Lunch breaks | 2-3 | Skip the scroll, ship instead |
| After bedtime (8:30-10pm) | 2-4 | Don't do this every night |

**Realistic income timeline:**
- Month 1-3: $0 (building, validating)
- Month 3-6: $100-500/mo (first customers)
- Month 6-12: $500-2000/mo (product-market fit)
- Year 2+: $2000-5000/mo (multiple products, compounding)

## The Rules

1. **Never sacrifice family time.** If your kid wants to play, close the laptop.
2. **Revenue over vanity metrics.** 10 paying customers > 10,000 Twitter followers.
3. **Automate everything.** If you're doing manual work repeatedly, you're doing it wrong.
4. **Charge from day one.** Free users don't validate anything.
5. **Set a "stop" time.** When the hour is up, stop. Consistency beats marathons.

- [ ] Write down 3 problems you've solved at work that others might pay for
- [ ] Set a weekly schedule: which hours are "side income" hours?
- [ ] Tell your partner your plan (transparency prevents resentment)

## Time Required

**This chapter:** 1 hour (time audit + brainstorming)
**Ongoing commitment:** 5-10 hours/week, scheduled like a recurring meeting

## Real Examples

- **Developer A:** Built a CSV-to-API tool during lunch breaks. 6 months to $1,200/mo. Still works full-time, spends 3 hours/week on maintenance.
- **Developer B:** Sells a Next.js SaaS starter kit. Created it over 2 months of early mornings. Now earns $3,000/mo with zero ongoing work.
- **Developer C:** Writes a paid newsletter about AWS costs. 2 hours/week writing. $800/mo from 400 subscribers at $2/mo.

## Pitfalls

- **Pitfall #1: Building for 6 months before launching.** You'll lose motivation and miss the market.
- **Pitfall #2: Optimizing before validating.** Nobody cares about your architecture if nobody's paying.
- **Pitfall #3: Comparing to full-time indie hackers.** They have 40+ hours/week. You have 7. Different game.
- **Pitfall #4: Hiding it from your family.** Resentment builds fast when your partner doesn't know why you're always on the laptop.
- **Pitfall #5: Ignoring your employment contract.** Check your IP clause. Build on your own time, on your own machine, in a different domain.
