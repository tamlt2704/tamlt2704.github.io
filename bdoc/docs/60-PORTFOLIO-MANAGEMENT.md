```
DIVERSIFY ACROSS:

1. ASSET CLASSES: stocks + bonds + REITs + cash
```

## 60.9 Dollar-cost averaging (DON'T invest all at once)

```
LUMP SUM vs DCA:

Lump sum: invest $20K today → if market drops tomorrow, you feel terrible
DCA: invest $5K per month for 4 months → average price, less timing risk

RECOMMENDED PLAN:
  Month 1: $7,000 (core ETFs: CSPX + IWDA)
  Month 2: $5,000 (Singapore positions: STI ETF + dividend stocks)
  Month 3: $5,000 (growth stocks + REITs)
  Month 4: $3,000 (bonds + top up positions)

WHY: Markets are volatile. DCA reduces the risk of buying at the absolute peak.
Exception: if market just crashed 20%+ → lump sum is better (buying cheap).
```

## PART 3: Position Sizing & Risk Management

## 60.10 The 1-2% rule

```
NEVER risk more than 1-2% of your portfolio on a SINGLE TRADE.

With $20,000 portfolio:
  1% risk = $200 maximum loss per trade
  2% risk = $400 maximum loss per trade

HOW TO CALCULATE POSITION SIZE:
  Risk per trade: $200
  Stop-loss distance: 10% below entry price
  Position size = Risk / Stop-loss distance = $200 / 10% = $2,000

  → Buy $2,000 of the stock
  → Set stop-loss at -10%
  → If stopped out, you lose $200 (1% of portfolio)
  → Portfolio survives even 10 consecutive losses (-10% drawdown, not wipeout)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  Without risk management: one bad trade can wipe out 50% of your capital
  With 1% rule: you need 50 consecutive losses to lose half (virtually impossible)
```

## 60.11 Stop-losses (protect your downside)

```
STOP-LOSS = automatic sell order if price drops to your limit

TYPES:
  Fixed percentage: sell if -8% to -12% from entry
  Technical level: sell if price breaks below key support
  Trailing stop: sell if price drops 10% from its HIGHEST point since you bought

EXAMPLE:
  Buy DBS at $36.00
  Stop-loss: $32.40 (-10%)
  → If DBS drops to $32.40, automatically sold. Loss capped at $3.60/share.
  → If DBS rises to $45, trailing stop at $40.50 (10% below high)

RULES:
  • Set the stop BEFORE you buy (not after you're losing)
  • NEVER move a stop-loss FURTHER away (that's called "hoping")
  • You CAN move it UP (lock in profits — trailing stop)
```

## 60.12 When to sell (exit strategy)

```
SELL WHEN:
  ✅ Hit your profit target (e.g., +20-30%)
  ✅ Stop-loss triggered (cut losses, no exceptions)
  ✅ Fundamental change (company deteriorated, thesis broken)
  ✅ Better opportunity (reallocate to higher-conviction position)
  ✅ Rebalancing (position grew too large relative to portfolio)

DON'T SELL BECAUSE:
  ❌ Stock dropped 5% today (normal volatility)
  ❌ Scary news headline (usually priced in by the time you read it)
  ❌ Everyone else is selling (panic selling = buying opportunity for others)
  ❌ You "feel" like it will drop (feelings ≠ analysis)
```

## PART 4: Trading Skills

## 60.13 Technical analysis basics

```
SUPPORT & RESISTANCE:

Price
$40 │────────────────── RESISTANCE ──────── (price bounces off this ceiling)
    │         ╱╲    ╱╲
    │        ╱  ╲  ╱  ╲    ╱╲
$35 │───────╱────╲╱────╲──╱──╲────────────
    │      ╱              ╱    ╲
    │     ╱              ╱      ╲
$30 │────╱──── SUPPORT ─╱────────╲──────── (price bounces off this floor)
    │
    └──────────────────────────────────── Time

Support: price level where buying pressure stops the decline
Resistance: price level where selling pressure stops the advance
Breakout: when price passes through support/resistance → big move
```

## 60.14 Key indicators (don't need many — just a few)

```
1. MOVING AVERAGES (trend direction):
   50-day MA: medium-term trend
   200-day MA: long-term trend
   Price ABOVE both → uptrend (bullish)
   Price BELOW both → downtrend (bearish)
   "Golden cross" (50 crosses above 200) → bullish signal
   "Death cross" (50 crosses below 200) → bearish signal

2. RSI (Relative Strength Index) — momentum:
   RSI > 70 → overbought (might pull back)
   RSI < 30 → oversold (might bounce)
   NOT a buy/sell signal alone! Confirmation needed.

3. VOLUME:
   Rising price + rising volume → strong move (conviction)
   Rising price + falling volume → weak move (might reverse)
   Big volume spike → institutional activity (pay attention)

IMPORTANT: Technical analysis works best for TIMING entries/exits.
           It does NOT tell you WHAT to buy (fundamentals do that).
```

## 60.15 Fundamental analysis (what to buy)

```
KEY METRICS TO CHECK BEFORE BUYING A STOCK:

VALUATION (is it cheap or expensive?):
  P/E Ratio: Price / Earnings Per Share
    < 15: potentially undervalued
    15-25: fairly valued
    > 30: expensive (better have high growth)
  
  P/B Ratio: Price / Book Value (for banks, REITs)
    < 1: trading below asset value (potentially cheap)
    > 3: premium (market expects growth)

GROWTH:
  Revenue growth: is the business growing?
  Earnings growth: is profit growing?
  Consistency: 5+ years of growth > one lucky year

QUALITY:
  ROE (Return on Equity): > 15% is excellent
  Debt/Equity: < 0.5 is conservative (for non-banks)
  Free cash flow: positive and growing (actual money generated)

DIVIDEND (for income stocks):
  Dividend yield: annual dividend / price (SG REITs typically 4-7%)
  Payout ratio: % of earnings paid as dividend (< 80% is sustainable)
  Dividend growth: is the dividend increasing year-over-year?

WHERE TO CHECK:
  • Yahoo Finance (free — key metrics + charts)
  • SimplyWall.St (visual analysis — free tier)
  • SGX StockFacts (Singapore-specific)
  • Annual reports (for serious analysis)
```

## PART 5: Trading Psychology

## 60.16 Emotional traps (the REAL reason most traders lose)

```
1. FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out):
   "Stock went up 50%! I need to buy NOW before it goes higher!"
   Reality: chasing rallies = buying at the top. You missed the easy money.
   Fix: if you missed it, wait for a pullback. There's always another trade.

2. LOSS AVERSION (holding losers too long):
   "It'll come back. I just need to wait."
   Reality: dead companies don't come back. -50% needs +100% to recover.
   Fix: honour your stop-loss. A small loss today prevents a disaster tomorrow.

3. OVERCONFIDENCE (after a winning streak):
   "I'm great at this! Let me put 50% on this one trade."
   Reality: 5 wins doesn't mean you're skilled. Could be luck + bull market.
   Fix: stick to position sizing rules. ALWAYS. Even when you feel invincible.

4. PANIC SELLING (market crash):
   "Everything is dropping! Sell everything NOW!"
   Reality: selling at the bottom locks in losses. Markets always recover (historically).
   Fix: if your thesis is intact and you're diversified → HOLD. Even add more.

5. REVENGE TRADING (after a loss):
   "I lost $500. I need to make it back RIGHT NOW with a bigger bet."
   Reality: this leads to bigger losses. Emotional decisions = bad decisions.
   Fix: after a loss, STOP. Don't trade for 24 hours. Review what went wrong calmly.
```

## 60.17 Rules for consistent performance

```
THE TRADING RULES (write these down, review weekly):

1. Never risk more than 2% per trade
2. Always have a stop-loss BEFORE entering
3. Don't chase — if you missed it, let it go
4. Cut losses quickly, let winners run
5. No more than 20% in any single stock
6. Keep a trading journal (write down WHY you bought/sold)
7. Review weekly: what worked? what didn't? why?
8. Don't check prices every 5 minutes (once/day is enough for positions)
9. If unsure → do nothing. Cash is a position.
10. The goal is CONSISTENCY, not one big win.
```

## PART 6: Ongoing Management

## 60.18 Rebalancing (quarterly or semi-annually)

```
Your target: 40% US ETF, 20% Global, 15% SG stocks, 15% REITs, 10% bonds

After 6 months: US ETF grew → now it's 50% of portfolio. SG stocks fell → only 10%.

REBALANCE:
  Sell some US ETF (trim winner)
  Buy more SG stocks (add to underweight)
  Back to target allocation

WHY: Rebalancing forces "buy low, sell high" automatically.
The thing that went UP → you sell some (taking profit).
The thing that went DOWN → you buy more (buying cheap).

HOW OFTEN: every 6-12 months OR when any position drifts >5% from target.
```

## Key takeaways

**Start simple, add complexity later.** A portfolio of 2-3 ETFs (CSPX + ES3 + A35) will outperform 90% of active traders. You can add individual stocks once you've learned the basics with real money (and real emotions).

**Risk management IS the skill.** Amateur traders focus on finding winners. Professional traders focus on limiting losers. A 50% win rate is fine if your winners are 2× your losers. You don't need to be right often — you need to not blow up.

**Time in the market > timing the market.** Over any 20-year period in history, stock markets have gone UP. The people who lost money are the ones who panicked out during crashes. Buy consistently, hold through volatility, let compounding work.

**Your biggest edge as a Singapore investor: no capital gains tax.** Compound freely without the government taking 15-40% of every profit (as they do in US, UK, Europe). This alone gives you a massive long-term advantage.
