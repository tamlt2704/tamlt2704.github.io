# Chapter 4: Both Hands — A Song With Both Hands

[← Chapter 3: First Chords](chapter-03-first-chords.md) | [Chapter 5: Major & Minor →](chapter-05-major-minor.md)

## The Problem

You can play a melody with your right hand. You can play chords with your left hand. But the moment you try to do both at the same time, your brain short-circuits. Your hands want to move together — pressing at the same time, lifting at the same time. They refuse to be independent.

Maya nods. "Everyone hits this wall. Your brain has spent your whole life coordinating your hands to do the same thing — typing, clapping, carrying boxes. Now you're asking them to do completely different things simultaneously. It's like patting your head and rubbing your stomach, except harder."

She pauses. "But here's the secret: you don't actually learn to do two things at once. You learn one thing so well it becomes automatic, then you add the other on top. Like walking and talking — you don't think about walking anymore."

## The Independence Trick

Here's the method that works:

1. **Learn the right hand part alone** — until you can play it without thinking
2. **Learn the left hand part alone** — until you can play it without thinking
3. **Combine at half speed** — painfully slow, but both hands together
4. **Gradually speed up** — only when the slow version is solid

This isn't a shortcut. It's the only way. Every pianist who plays with both hands went through this process. The difference between a beginner and a pro is that the pro has automated thousands of patterns.

## Left Hand Patterns

Your left hand doesn't have to play full chords on every beat. Here are four common patterns, from simplest to most complex:

### Pattern 1: Whole Notes (Easiest)

Left hand plays one note (the root of the chord) and holds it for the entire measure:

```
Left hand:   C───────────────    F───────────────    G───────────────
             (hold 4 beats)      (hold 4 beats)      (hold 4 beats)
```

Just one finger. One note. Hold it. This gives your right hand all the attention.

### Pattern 2: Half Notes

Left hand plays the root twice per measure:

```
Left hand:   C───── C─────      F───── F─────      G───── G─────
             beat 1  beat 3      beat 1  beat 3      beat 1  beat 3
```

A little more rhythmic energy, still very simple.

### Pattern 3: Chord on Beat 1

Left hand plays the full chord once at the start of each measure, then rests:

```
Left hand:   C-E-G─────────     F-A-C─────────     G-B-D─────────
             beat 1 (hold)       beat 1 (hold)       beat 1 (hold)
```

This is what you practiced in Chapter 3. One chord per measure, held for 4 beats.

### Pattern 4: Broken Chord (Arpeggio)

Left hand plays the chord notes one at a time:

```
Left hand:   C    E    G    E       F    A    C    A
             1    2    3    4       1    2    3    4
Finger:      5    3    1    3       5    3    1    3
```

This sounds flowing and elegant. It's harder because your left hand is now playing a rhythm, not just holding notes. Save this for later in the week.

## Your First Both-Hands Piece: "Twinkle Twinkle"

We'll build this up in layers. Don't skip ahead.

### Step 1: Right Hand Alone

You already know this from Chapter 1. Play it until it's effortless:

```
Right hand melody (C position, starting on Middle C):

C  C  G  G  A  A  G─     F  F  E  E  D  D  C─
1  1  5  5  5  5  5      4  4  3  3  2  2  1

G  G  F  F  E  E  D─     G  G  F  F  E  E  D─
5  5  4  4  3  3  2      5  5  4  4  3  3  2

C  C  G  G  A  A  G─     F  F  E  E  D  D  C─
1  1  5  5  5  5  5      4  4  3  3  2  2  1
```

Note: The "─" means hold that note longer (half note = 2 beats).

Play this 5 times without mistakes before moving on.

### Step 2: Left Hand Alone (Pattern 1 — Whole Notes)

Your left hand plays single notes, one octave below Middle C:

```
Left hand (whole notes):

Measure:  | 1        | 2        | 3        | 4        |
Note:     | C─────── | C─────── | F─────── | C─────── |
Finger:   | 5        | 5        | 2        | 5        |

Measure:  | 5        | 6        | 7        | 8        |
Note:     | C─────── | F─────── | C─────── | G─────── |
Finger:   | 5        | 2        | 5        | 1        |

Measure:  | 9        | 10       | 11       | 12       |
Note:     | C─────── | C─────── | F─────── | C─────── |
Finger:   | 5        | 5        | 2        | 5        |
```

Just three notes: C (finger 5), F (finger 2), G (finger 1). Hold each for a full measure.

Play this 5 times without mistakes.

### Step 3: Combine — Extremely Slowly

Now both hands together. Count "1 - 2 - 3 - 4" out loud. Very slowly.

```
Measure 1:                          Measure 2:
Right: C  C  G  G                   Right: A  A  G─────
Left:  C──────────── (hold)         Left:  C──────────── (hold)
Count: 1  2  3  4                   Count: 1  2  3  4

Measure 3:                          Measure 4:
Right: F  F  E  E                   Right: D  D  C─────
Left:  F──────────── (hold)         Left:  C──────────── (hold)
Count: 1  2  3  4                   Count: 1  2  3  4
```

**What will happen:** Your right hand will freeze when your left hand moves to a new note. Or your left hand will accidentally follow your right hand's rhythm. This is normal. Go slower.

**The rule:** If you can't play it slowly, you can't play it fast. Speed comes from accuracy, not from trying harder.

### Step 4: Build Up Gradually

Once measures 1-4 are solid, add measures 5-8. Then 9-12. Don't try to play the whole song until each section works.

## Common Problems (And Fixes)

| Problem | What's Happening | Fix |
|---------|-----------------|-----|
| Both hands play same rhythm | Brain defaults to synchronization | Practice left hand alone until it's boring |
| Right hand slows down at chord changes | Attention shifts to left hand | Make left hand changes smaller (use Pattern 1) |
| Left hand gets louder than right | Tension from concentration | Consciously play left hand softer |
| You lose your place | Trying to read both parts at once | Memorize one hand completely first |
| It sounds choppy | Lifting left hand to move | Keep left hand notes connected (legato) |

## Exercise 1: The Simplest Both-Hands Pattern

Before tackling a full song, try this. Right hand plays a five-finger pattern while left hand holds one note:

```
Right hand:  C  D  E  F  G  F  E  D  C
Left hand:   C──────────────────────────── (hold entire time)
```

Your left hand does literally nothing except hold one key down. This trains your brain to let the hands operate independently.

Do this 5 times. Then change the left hand note:

```
Right hand:  C  D  E  F  G  F  E  D  C
Left hand:   G──────────────────────────── (hold entire time)
```

Same right hand, different left hand note. Your brain has to accept that the hands are doing different things.

## Exercise 2: Left Hand Moves Once

Now the left hand changes one time during the pattern:

```
Right hand:  C  D  E  F  │  G  F  E  D  C
Left hand:   C────────────│  G──────────────
                          ↑
                    Left hand moves here
```

The vertical line marks where your left hand switches from C to G. Practice hitting that transition point cleanly — left hand moves while right hand continues without hesitation.

## Exercise 3: Alternating Hands

This builds coordination without requiring simultaneous playing:

```
Beat:    1     2     3     4     1     2     3     4
Right:   C     -     E     -     G     -     E     -
Left:    -     C     -     E     -     G     -     E
```

Hands take turns. Right on beats 1 and 3, left on beats 2 and 4. Like a conversation between your hands.

Once this is comfortable, try overlapping — left hand holds while right hand plays:

```
Beat:    1     2     3     4
Right:   C     D     E     F
Left:    C─────────────────── (hold through all 4 beats)
```

## Exercise 4: "Mary Had a Little Lamb" — Both Hands

A simpler song for your first full both-hands piece:

```
Right hand:  E  D  C  D  │  E  E  E─  │  D  D  D─  │  E  G  G─
Left hand:   C────────────│  C──────────│  G──────────│  C──────────
Finger (L):  5            │  5          │  1          │  5

Right hand:  E  D  C  D  │  E  E  E  E │  D  D  E  D │  C─────
Left hand:   C────────────│  C──────────│  G──────────│  C──────
Finger (L):  5            │  5          │  1          │  5
```

**Practice order:**
1. Right hand alone — 5 times perfectly
2. Left hand alone — 5 times perfectly (it's just C and G alternating)
3. Both hands, measures 1-4 only, very slowly
4. Both hands, measures 5-8 only, very slowly
5. Both hands, full song, still slowly
6. Gradually increase speed over several days

## The Speed Trap

Maya watches you struggle through the first four measures at a crawl.

"I know it feels ridiculous playing this slowly. But here's what happens if you rush: you practice mistakes. Your muscles memorize the wrong thing. Then you have to unlearn it, which takes three times longer than learning it right the first time."

She holds up her phone. "Set a tempo. Start at 60 BPM — that's one beat per second. Don't increase speed until you can play it three times in a row without a single mistake. Then go to 65. Then 70. Patience is the skill."

**The speed ladder:**

```
Week 1:  60 BPM  (one beat per second — feels absurdly slow)
Week 2:  70 BPM  (starting to feel like music)
Week 3:  80 BPM  (comfortable, flowing)
Week 4:  90 BPM  (the actual tempo of most simple songs)
```

**Rules for increasing speed:**
1. Play the piece 3 times in a row with zero mistakes at current tempo
2. Increase by 5 BPM
3. If you make mistakes at the new tempo, drop back down
4. Never jump more than 10 BPM in one session

## Exercise 5: The "Freeze Frame" Technique

When you get stuck at a particular spot (usually where the left hand changes), use this method:

1. Play up to the problem spot and STOP
2. Freeze both hands in position
3. Look at where each hand needs to go next
4. Move both hands to the new position (without playing)
5. Now play from one beat before the problem through one beat after

Repeat the problem spot 10 times in isolation. Then play the full passage. The trouble spot will feel easier because you've given it extra attention.

Maya: "Professionals don't practice pieces start to finish. They find the hard spots and drill those. The easy parts take care of themselves."

## What You Learned

- **The independence trick** — learn each hand separately, then combine at half speed
- **Four left hand patterns** — whole notes, half notes, chord on beat 1, broken chord
- **Both-hands coordination** — starts with one hand holding while the other moves
- **The speed trap** — slow and correct beats fast and sloppy every time
- **Two songs with both hands** — "Mary Had a Little Lamb" and "Twinkle Twinkle"
