## The Problem

You're sitting at a keyboard. There are 88 keys (or 61, or 49). White ones, black ones. They all look the same. Where do you even start?

Maya sits next to you. "See the black keys? They're not random. They come in groups."

You look. She's right. There's a pattern: **groups of 2** and **groups of 3**, alternating across the entire keyboard.

```
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██     ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██     ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
  ┌──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┐
  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │
  │ C│ D│ E│ F│ G│ A│ B│ C│ D│ E│ F│ G│ A│ B│
  └──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┘
       2 blacks        3 blacks       2 blacks
```

"The group of 2 black keys — the white key just to the LEFT of that group is always C. Every single time, anywhere on the keyboard."

## The Black Key Pattern

This is the single most important visual landmark on the piano:

```
Group of 2:  ██ ██        → C is immediately left of this group
Group of 3:  ██ ██ ██     → F is immediately left of this group
```

Find any group of 2 black keys. The white key to their left is C. The one between them? D. The one to their right? E.

Find any group of 3 black keys. The white key to their left is F. Then G, A, B — and you're back to C again.

**The musical alphabet has 7 letters: C D E F G A B. Then it repeats.**

That's it. Seven notes. Over and over. Higher as you go right, lower as you go left.

## Finding Middle C

Middle C is the C closest to the center of your keyboard.

- **88-key piano:** It's the 4th C from the left, roughly in the middle. Often has a brand logo above it.
- **61-key keyboard:** It's the 3rd C from the left, slightly left of center.
- **49-key keyboard:** It's the 3rd C from the left, at the center.

Sit centered in front of your keyboard. Find the group of 2 black keys nearest the middle. The white key to the left of that group is Middle C.

Press it. That's your home base. Everything starts here.

## The C-D-E-F-G-A-B Sequence

Starting from Middle C, play each white key going right:

```
C → D → E → F → G → A → B → C (one octave higher)
```

Say the letter names out loud as you play them. Do this three times.

Now go backwards:

```
C → B → A → G → F → E → D → C (back to Middle C)
```

Three times. Say the names.

**What you're hearing:** This is the C major scale — the most fundamental sound in Western music. Every white key from C to C. No sharps, no flats. Pure and simple.

## Your First Melody: "Mary Had a Little Lamb"

You know enough to play a song. Here it is using letter names:

```
E  D  C  D  E  E  E
Ma-ry had a  lit-tle lamb

D  D  D     E  G  G
Lit-tle lamb, lit-tle lamb

E  D  C  D  E  E  E  E
Ma-ry had a  lit-tle lamb its

D  D  E  D  C
fleece was white as snow
```

Play it with any finger. Don't worry about which finger goes where — that's Chapter 2. Just find the right keys and play the melody.

**Troubleshooting:**
- Sounds wrong? Make sure you're starting on E (the white key to the RIGHT of the group of 2 black keys).
- Can't find G? It's the second white key in the group of 3 (between the first and second black keys of the group of 3).

## The Octave: Same Note, Different Height

Press Middle C. Now find the next C to the right (skip 7 white keys). Press it.

They sound like the same note — just higher. That's an **octave**. "Oct" means 8 — count the white keys from C to C inclusive: C(1) D(2) E(3) F(4) G(5) A(6) B(7) C(8).

Every note repeats in octaves. The C two octaves above Middle C sounds like Middle C, just brighter and thinner. The C below Middle C sounds like Middle C, just darker and fuller.

This is why the piano has 88 keys but only 7 note names. It's the same 7 notes repeated across 7+ octaves.

## The Black Keys (Preview)

You've been playing only white keys. The black keys are the notes "between" the white keys — sharps (♯) and flats (♭).

- The black key between C and D? That's C♯ (C sharp) or D♭ (D flat). Same key, two names.
- The black key between F and G? That's F♯ or G♭.

Notice: there's NO black key between E and F, or between B and C. Those white keys are already right next to each other — there's no "between" to fill.

We won't use black keys until Chapter 5. For now, the white keys give you everything you need.

## Exercise: Note Scavenger Hunt

Without looking at labels, find these notes as fast as you can:

1. The G above Middle C
2. The F below Middle C
3. The highest C on your keyboard
4. The lowest E on your keyboard
5. The A closest to the center

**How to find any note:**
1. Find a group of 2 or 3 black keys nearby
2. Use the landmark: C is left of the 2-group, F is left of the 3-group
3. Count from the landmark to your target

Time yourself. Do it again tomorrow. You'll be twice as fast.

## Exercise: Three Simple Melodies

### "Hot Cross Buns"
```
E  D  C  (rest)  E  D  C  (rest)
Hot cross buns,   hot cross buns,

C  C  D  D  E  D  C
One a pen-ny two a pen-ny hot cross buns
```

### "Twinkle Twinkle Little Star"
```
C  C  G  G  A  A  G
Twin-kle twin-kle lit-tle star

F  F  E  E  D  D  C

```

### "Ode to Joy" (first line)
```
E  E  F  G  G  F  E  D
C  C  D  E  E  D  D
```

Play each one until you can do it without looking at the letters. That's the beginning of memorization — and memorization is the beginning of music feeling natural.

## What You Learned

- **The black key pattern** — groups of 2 and 3, your visual landmarks
- **C is left of the 2-group, F is left of the 3-group** — the only rule you need to find any note
- **The musical alphabet** — C D E F G A B, repeating forever
- **Middle C** — your home base, center of the keyboard
- **Octaves** — same note, different height, 8 white keys apart
- **Three melodies** — proof that you can play music right now

Maya watches you stumble through "Twinkle Twinkle." You hit a wrong note, backtrack, find the right one.

"See? You're already playing. Now let's talk about which fingers to use — because right now you're poking at it like you're typing an email."
