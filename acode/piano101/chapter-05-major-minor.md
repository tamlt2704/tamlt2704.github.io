## The Problem

Every song you've played so far sounds... happy. Bright. Cheerful. But music isn't always cheerful. Some songs are sad, mysterious, dark, haunting. You want to play something that sounds melancholy — but all your chords sound like sunshine.

Maya plays a C major chord. Bright. Then she lowers one finger by a single key — and suddenly it sounds completely different. Darker. Sadder. Like the same room with the lights dimmed.

"One note. That's all it takes to change the entire mood of a chord. One note, one half step lower."

## Major vs Minor: The Third

Remember the C major chord? C - E - G. Three notes, skip pattern.

Now play this: **C - E♭ - G**.

```
C Major:                        C Minor:
                                     ▼ E♭ (black key)
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██          ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██          ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
  ┌──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┐        ┌──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┐
  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │        │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │
  │ C│  │ E│  │ G│  │  │        │ C│  │  │  │ G│  │  │
  └──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┘        └──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┘
   ▲      ▲      ▲                ▲   ▲         ▲
   1      3      5                1   E♭         5
                                      (3)
```

The only difference: the middle note (E) moved down one key to E♭ — the black key just to the left of E.

Play them back to back:
- C major (C-E-G): bright, happy, resolved
- C minor (C-E♭-G): dark, sad, mysterious

**That middle note is called "the third."** In a major chord, the third is 4 half steps above the root. In a minor chord, it's 3 half steps above the root. One half step changes everything.

## Sharps and Flats — Finally

You've been playing only white keys until now. Time to meet the black keys properly.

**A sharp (♯)** raises a note by one key to the right (up):
- C♯ = the black key to the right of C
- F♯ = the black key to the right of F

**A flat (♭)** lowers a note by one key to the left (down):
- E♭ = the black key to the left of E
- B♭ = the black key to the left of B

**A half step** = the distance from any key to the very next key (white or black). It's the smallest distance on the piano.

```
Half steps on the keyboard:

    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
  ┌──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┐
  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │
  │ C│ D│ E│ F│ G│ A│ B│ C│
  └──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┘

  C to C♯ = half step (white to black)
  C♯ to D = half step (black to white)
  E to F  = half step (white to white — no black key between them!)
  B to C  = half step (white to white — no black key between them!)
```

**Key insight:** E-F and B-C are natural half steps. There's no black key between them because they're already as close as two notes can be.

## Your First Black Key: E♭

Find E on the keyboard (right of the 2-black-key group). Now move one key to the left — that's E♭. It's the black key between D and E.

Press it. It sounds just like any other note. Black keys aren't special or scary — they're just notes that happen to be raised up for your fingers to find easily.

**Fingering for C minor chord:**
- Finger 1 (thumb): C
- Finger 3 (middle): E♭ — your finger plays on the black key
- Finger 5 (pinky): G

Your hand shape stays the same. Finger 3 just reaches slightly forward and up to the black key. Keep your wrist level.

## A Minor: All White Keys

Here's a minor chord that uses no black keys at all:

**A minor: A - C - E (fingers 1-3-5)**

```
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
    ██  ██     ██  ██  ██
  ┌──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┬──┐
  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │  │
  │  │  │  │  │ A│  │ C│  │ E│
  └──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┴──┘
                  ▲      ▲      ▲
                  1      3      5
```

Wait — all white keys? How is this minor?

Count the half steps from A to C: A → A♯ → B → C = 3 half steps. That's a minor third. The "skip pattern" from Chapter 3 doesn't always produce major chords — it depends on where you start. Starting on A with all white keys naturally gives you a minor chord.

Play A minor. It sounds sad, gentle, introspective. It's one of the most common chords in pop music.

## D Minor and E Minor

Two more minor chords using only white keys:

**D minor: D - F - A (fingers 1-3-5)**

```
D to F = 3 half steps (D → D♯ → E → F) = minor third ✓
```

**E minor: E - G - B (fingers 1-3-5)**

```
E to G = 3 half steps (E → F → F♯ → G) = minor third ✓
```

Both all white keys. Both naturally minor because of where the half steps fall.

Play all four minor chords in sequence:

```
Am (A-C-E) → Dm (D-F-A) → Em (E-G-B) → Am (A-C-E)
```

Hear that? Dark, flowing, melancholy. A completely different emotional world from C-F-G-C.

## The Emotional Palette

| Chord Type | Sound | Used For |
|-----------|-------|----------|
| Major | Bright, happy, strong | Joy, triumph, energy, resolution |
| Minor | Dark, sad, mysterious | Melancholy, longing, tension, beauty |

But it's not that simple. Minor doesn't always mean "sad" — it can mean mysterious, cool, sophisticated, or intense. And major doesn't always mean "happy" — it can mean powerful, triumphant, or peaceful.

Maya: "Don't think of it as happy vs sad. Think of it as light vs shadow. You need both to make a painting interesting."

## Exercise 1: Major vs Minor Comparison

Play each pair back to back. Listen to the mood shift:

```
C major (C-E-G)    →  C minor (C-E♭-G)
F major (F-A-C)    →  F minor (F-A♭-C)
G major (G-B-D)    →  G minor (G-B♭-D)
A major (A-C♯-E)   →  A minor (A-C-E)
```

For F minor: A♭ is the black key to the left of A.
For G minor: B♭ is the black key to the left of B.
For A major: C♯ is the black key to the right of C.

Notice: to make any major chord minor, lower the middle note by one half step. To make any minor chord major, raise the middle note by one half step.

## Exercise 2: Same Melody, Different Mood

Play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" melody with your right hand. But change the left hand chords:

**Happy version (major chords):**
```
Right hand:  E  D  C  D  E  E  E     D  D  D     E  G  G
Left hand:   C major──────────────    G major───   C major──
```

**Sad version (minor chords):**
```
Right hand:  E  D  C  D  E  E  E     D  D  D     E  G  G
Left hand:   C minor──────────────    G minor───   C minor──
```

Same melody. Completely different feeling. The chords underneath color everything.

## Exercise 3: Minor Chord Switching

Practice switching between minor chords smoothly:

```
| Am  Am  Am  Am | Dm  Dm  Dm  Dm | Em  Em  Em  Em | Am  Am  Am  Am |
  (A-C-E)          (D-F-A)          (E-G-B)          (A-C-E)
```

This is the i-iv-v progression in A minor. It works just like I-IV-V in major — these three chords are the backbone of minor-key songs.

## Exercise 4: Mixing Major and Minor

Many songs mix major and minor chords. Try these progressions:

```
Pattern 1:  Am  F   C   G       (minor start, major finish — hopeful)
Pattern 2:  C   Am  F   G       (major start, minor dip — bittersweet)
Pattern 3:  Am  F   Am  Em      (all minor except F — dark with a spark)
Pattern 4:  C   G   Am  F       (the "pop progression" — used in hundreds of songs)
```

Pattern 4 is especially important. Play it on repeat. If it sounds familiar, that's because it IS familiar — "Let It Be," "No Woman No Cry," "With or Without You," and countless others use this exact progression.

## Chord Notation: How Minor Is Written

| Written As | Means | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Am or Amin | A minor | A-C-E |
| Dm or Dmin | D minor | D-F-A |
| Em or Emin | E minor | E-G-B |
| Cm or Cmin | C minor | C-E♭-G |
| Fm or Fmin | F minor | F-A♭-C |

The lowercase "m" after the letter means minor. No "m" means major.

## What You Learned

- **The third** — the middle note of a chord determines major or minor
- **Major chord** — root + 4 half steps + 3 half steps (bright)
- **Minor chord** — root + 3 half steps + 4 half steps (dark)
- **Sharps and flats** — raise or lower a note by one key
- **Half step** — the smallest distance between two keys
- **Am, Dm, Em** — minor chords using only white keys
- **Cm** — your first chord with a black key (E♭)
- **The pop progression** — C-G-Am-F (used in hundreds of songs)
