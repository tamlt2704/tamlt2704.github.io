# Chapter 2: Finger Technique — Stop Poking

## The Problem

You can find notes. You can play melodies. But you're using one finger — or maybe two — jabbing at keys like a woodpecker. It works, but it's slow, it's tense, and it falls apart the moment a melody moves fast.

Maya holds up her hand. "Five fingers. Five notes within reach without moving your hand. That's the whole trick. You don't chase notes — you position your hand so the notes are already under your fingers."

## Finger Numbers

Every pianist on Earth uses the same numbering:

```
         Left Hand                    Right Hand

         5 4 3 2 1                    1 2 3 4 5
         │ │ │ │ │                    │ │ │ │ │
         pinky                        thumb
         ring                              pinky
         middle                       
         index                        
         thumb                        
```

| Finger | Number | Both Hands |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Thumb | 1 | Strongest, shortest |
| Index | 2 | Most independent |
| Middle | 3 | Longest, good reach |
| Ring | 4 | Weakest, shares tendons with 3 and 5 |
| Pinky | 5 | Small but important for range |

**The rule:** Thumb is always 1. Pinky is always 5. Doesn't matter which hand.

## The C Position

Place your right hand on the keyboard:

```
Finger:    1    2    3    4    5
Note:      C    D    E    F    G
```

Your thumb (1) on Middle C. Index (2) on D. Middle (3) on E. Ring (4) on F. Pinky (5) on G.

Each finger owns one key. No reaching, no stretching. Five notes, five fingers, zero movement.

Now your left hand, one octave lower:

```
Finger:    5    4    3    2    1
Note:      C    D    E    F    G
```

Left pinky (5) on the C below Middle C. Left thumb (1) on G.

**Notice:** The numbering mirrors. Both thumbs point toward the center of the keyboard. Both pinkies point outward.

## Hand Shape

Maya cups her hand like she's holding a tennis ball. "This. Always this."

**The correct shape:**
- Fingers curved, not flat
- Knuckles slightly raised (you could fit a marble under your palm)
- Wrist level with the forearm — not drooping, not arched up
- Fingertips contact the keys, not finger pads
- Thumb plays with its side corner, slightly angled

**Common mistakes:**

| Mistake | What Happens | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Flat fingers | Slipping, no control | Curve like holding a ball |
| Collapsed knuckles | Weak tone, strain | Keep knuckles as the highest point |
| Wrist too low | Tension in forearm | Raise wrist to forearm level |
| Wrist too high | Fingers lose power | Lower until level |
| Thumb flat under palm | Can't move independently | Thumb plays on its corner |

## Exercise 1: The Five-Finger Pattern (Right Hand)

Play C-D-E-F-G with fingers 1-2-3-4-5. Then come back: G-F-E-D-C with 5-4-3-2-1.

```
Up:    C  D  E  F  G
       1  2  3  4  5

Down:  G  F  E  D  C
       5  4  3  2  1
```

Rules:
- One note per beat. Steady. No rushing.
- Each note should sound the same volume (this is harder than it sounds — finger 4 will be quieter).
- Lift each finger after playing — don't hold notes down while playing the next one.
- Keep your wrist still. Movement comes from the fingers, not the hand.

Do this 5 times. Then switch to left hand (same notes, one octave lower, fingers 5-4-3-2-1 going up).

## Exercise 2: Evenness Training

Your ring finger (4) is weak. Your pinky (5) is weak. This exercise targets them.

```
Right hand, repeat each line 4 times:

3  4  3  4  3  4  3  4    (E-F alternating)
4  5  4  5  4  5  4  5    (F-G alternating)
3  4  5  4  3  4  5  4    (E-F-G-F pattern)
```

Listen for volume differences. Finger 4 will want to be softer. Push it to match finger 3. This takes weeks — that's normal.

## Exercise 3: "Mary Had a Little Lamb" with Finger Numbers

Remember this melody? Now play it with correct fingering:

```
Note:    E  D  C  D  E  E  E
Finger:  3  2  1  2  3  3  3

Note:    D  D  D     E  G  G
Finger:  2  2  2     3  5  5

Note:    E  D  C  D  E  E  E  E
Finger:  3  2  1  2  3  3  3  3

Note:    D  D  E  D  C
Finger:  2  2  3  2  1
```

**The difference:** Your hand stays in one position the entire song. No hunting. No looking down. Your fingers know where the notes are because each finger owns its key.

## Exercise 4: Independence Drill

Play these patterns. The goal is smooth, connected sound — each note flowing into the next without gaps or overlaps.

```
Pattern A:  1  3  5  3  1  3  5  3   (C-E-G-E repeating)
Pattern B:  1  2  3  4  5  4  3  2   (up and down smoothly)
Pattern C:  1  5  2  4  3  3  4  2   (random-feeling, builds independence)
```

Pattern C is the hardest. Your brain has to think about each finger individually instead of relying on the comfortable 1-2-3-4-5 sequence.

## The Legato Touch

"Legato" means smooth and connected. One note flows into the next with no silence between them.

**How to do it:**
1. Press key with finger 1 (C sounds)
2. While C is still sounding, press key with finger 2 (D sounds)
3. The instant D sounds, release finger 1
4. While D is still sounding, press key with finger 3 (E sounds)
5. The instant E sounds, release finger 2

It's like walking — one foot lands before the other lifts. There's always one note sounding, never two, never zero.

Play the five-finger pattern legato. Then play it **staccato** (short and detached — press and immediately release, like the key is hot). Feel the difference.

## The Gravity Drop

Maya demonstrates. She lifts her hand about 6 inches above the keys, completely relaxed. Then she drops it — letting gravity do the work — and a full, round note rings out.

"That's how you get a good tone. You're not pushing the key down. You're letting your arm weight fall through your fingertip into the key."

**Try it:**
1. Arm relaxed, hand in curved position
2. Lift from the wrist about 4-6 inches
3. Drop onto Middle C with finger 3
4. Let the weight of your arm produce the sound
5. No extra push. No tension. Just gravity.

The note should sound full and warm. If it's thin or harsh, you're adding tension. If it's too quiet, lift higher.

## Common Early Frustrations

**"My pinky is useless."** Everyone's is at first. It gets stronger. The evenness exercises help.

**"I keep hitting two keys at once."** Your fingers are too flat. Curve more — play on the tips.

**"My wrist hurts after 10 minutes."** You're tense. Shake your hands out. Check your wrist height. Take breaks every 10 minutes for the first few weeks.

**"I can't stop looking at my hands."** That's fine for now. You'll gradually look less as muscle memory builds. Don't force it.

## What You Learned

- **Finger numbers** — 1 (thumb) through 5 (pinky), same for both hands
- **C position** — five fingers covering C-D-E-F-G, no hand movement needed
- **Hand shape** — curved fingers, raised knuckles, level wrist
- **Legato** — smooth connected playing, one note flows into the next
- **Gravity drop** — arm weight produces tone, not finger force
- **Finger 4 is weak** — and that's normal, it gets better with targeted practice
